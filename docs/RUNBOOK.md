# Runbook

What to do when one of Kritzel's alerts fires. Every alert rule links to its section here through its `runbook_url`. The metrics, dashboards and limits mentioned are described in [OBSERVABILITY.md](OBSERVABILITY.md).

The alerts are defined in `deploy/helm/kritzel-observability/files/prometheus/rules/kritzel-alerts.yaml` (thresholds) and `deploy/helm/kritzel-observability/files/prometheus/rules/sloth-kritzel.yaml` (burn rates of the service level objectives, generated by Sloth). Prometheus evaluates them. The bundled stack has no Alertmanager, so an alert notifies nobody: it shows up in Grafana, and `ALERTS{alertstate="firing"}` in Explore lists what fires now.

**Owner.** Every alert carries `owner: operator`. That is whoever runs the instance. Kritzel has no vendor on call; if the cause is a defect in Kritzel, the operator works around it and reports it to the project.

## Severities

| Severity   | Used by                                                                                                                                | What it asks for                                                             |
| ---------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `critical` | The page variant of a burn-rate alert                                                                                                  | Look now. Players are affected and the error budget will be gone within days |
| `warning`  | The ticket variant of a burn-rate alert; `KritzelTelemetryMissing`, `KritzelServerFull`, `KritzelEventLoopLag`, `KritzelHeapNearLimit` | Look the same working day                                                    |
| `info`     | `KritzelRejectedMessageSurge`, `KritzelRateLimitSurge`, `KritzelTelemetryDropped`                                                      | Worth a look when convenient. Often needs no action                          |

### Page and ticket

Sloth generates up to two alerts per objective. They share the alert name and differ in the labels `sloth_severity` and `severity`.

An objective of 99.9 % leaves an error budget of 0.1 % of the events over 30 days; 99 % leaves 1 %. The burn rate says how fast the budget is being spent: 1 means it lasts exactly the 30 days.

| Variant | Labels                                          | Fires when the error ratio exceeds                                                                | At that rate the 30-day budget lasts |
| ------- | ----------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| Page    | `sloth_severity="page"`, `severity="critical"`  | 14.4 times the budget over both 5 minutes and 1 hour, or 6 times over both 30 minutes and 6 hours | about 2 days, or 5 days              |
| Ticket  | `sloth_severity="ticket"`, `severity="warning"` | 3 times the budget over both 2 hours and 1 day, or 1 time over both 6 hours and 3 days            | 10 days, or the full period          |

For a 99.9 % objective, 14.4 times the budget is an error ratio of 1.44 %; for a 99 % objective it is 14.4 %.

"Page" is Sloth's word for the urgent variant. Nothing in this stack pages anyone.

Two things to keep in mind:

- **The burn-rate alerts have no minimum-volume guard.** On a quiet instance one bad event can be a large share of a window. Check the absolute number of events before you act.
- **The local stack keeps 24 hours of data by default.** The one-day and three-day windows and the 30-day budget then reflect only what is retained.

## Before you act

Not everything that looks like a problem is one.

- **Expected player mistakes are not incidents.** A join with a wrong room code, to a full room, by a kicked player or with an invalid nickname is labelled `kritzel_validity="excluded"` and counts against nothing. A guesser who tries to draw is rejected with `not_allowed`. Wrong guesses, an unknown room (404) and a single rate-limit hit are ordinary.
- **A stopped demo environment is not an incident.** After `pnpm obs:down` nothing runs and nothing is evaluated. If the game is stopped on purpose while the stack keeps running, or telemetry is switched off on purpose, `KritzelTelemetryMissing` fires by design.
- **Synthetic traffic causes alerts on purpose.** The `server-full` scenario of the traffic generator is meant to fire `KritzelServerFull` and `KritzelApiAvailabilityBurn`. Check whether someone is running it.
- **Telemetry describes the server.** It contains no addresses, nicknames or room codes. To find out _who_ sends something, use the access log of your reverse proxy.

## General recovery

1. **Is the game up?** `GET /healthz` answers `200 {"status":"ok"}` when it is. This check does not depend on telemetry.
2. **Read the game's stdout.** One JSON object per line. `listening` shows the configuration, `telemetry export problem` reports a failing export.
3. **Look at the Global Overview dashboard** (`kritzel-overview`) for rooms, players, errors and the burn rate, then at the dashboard the alert's section names.
4. **Restart only when it helps.** A restart ends every running game: rooms live in the memory of one process ([ADR 0001](adr/0001-single-process-in-memory-state.md)) and are gone afterwards. On `SIGTERM` the server tells every participant; players see "The server is restarting" and have to create a new room. Games cut short this way are counted in `kritzel_games_aborted_total{kritzel_reason="shutdown"}`. When you can choose the moment, pick one with few rooms.
5. **A change of configuration needs a restart** too, because the variables are read at start-up.
6. **Fixing the telemetry path does not need a restart of the game.** Restart or repair the collector or the backend; the game keeps exporting and delivers again once the path works. Note that `pnpm obs:down` stops the game together with the stack. To restart a single service of the local stack:

   ```bash
   docker compose --env-file .env.observability -f docker-compose.yml -f docker-compose.observability.yml restart otel-collector
   ```

7. **Run one instance.** A second instance would not see the first one's rooms; load cannot be spread that way.

## KritzelTelemetryMissing

**Severity:** warning. **Rule:** `absent(kritzel_rooms_active)` for 5 minutes.

**What it means.** Prometheus has received no sample of `kritzel_rooms_active` for five minutes. The game reports this gauge at every export, also at zero, so its absence means the game is not exporting or the path game → collector → Prometheus is broken. Players may be unaffected. While this alert fires, no other alert and no objective can see anything.

**Likely causes.**

- The game is down, or restarts in a loop.
- Telemetry is off: `TELEMETRY_ENABLED` is not `true`, `OTEL_SDK_DISABLED=true`, or `OTEL_METRICS_EXPORTER=none`.
- The collector is down or unreachable, or `OTEL_EXPORTER_OTLP_ENDPOINT` points to the wrong place.
- The collector cannot write to Prometheus.
- The game was stopped on purpose.

**How to check.**

1. `curl -fsS http://127.0.0.1:3000/healthz` on the host of the game. No answer: the game is down; this is an outage, not a telemetry problem.
2. `pnpm obs:status` (local stack) or your platform's container list: which containers run, which data sources are healthy.
3. The game's stdout. The `listening` line must show `"telemetry":true`. A `telemetry export problem` line names the export error.
4. **Runtime & Infrastructure** dashboard, row _Telemetry pipeline_ and _Backends_: _Reachable_, _Collector: received_, _Collector: refused or failed_, _Collector: exported_, _Collector: export queues_. If these are empty as well, the collector itself is down.
5. In Explore: `kritzel_rooms_active` and `up`.

**What to do.**

- Game down: bring it back (see [General recovery](#general-recovery)) and read its last log lines for the reason.
- Telemetry path broken: repair the collector, the endpoint or the backend. The game does not need a restart unless its own variables change.
- Stopped or switched off on purpose: nothing.

**Owner:** the operator of the instance.

## KritzelServerFull

**Severity:** warning. **Rule:** at least one room creation refused with reason `server_full` in the last 10 minutes, for 1 minute.

**What it means.** `MAX_ROOMS` live rooms exist, and `POST /api/rooms` answers `503`. Players cannot create rooms; existing rooms and games continue. Every refusal also counts against the `api-availability` objective, so `KritzelApiAvailabilityBurn` may follow.

**Likely causes.**

- More demand than `MAX_ROOMS` allows (default 500), or `MAX_ROOMS` was set low.
- Rooms created in bulk. One address may create ten rooms per minute, and a room nobody joins is disposed after five minutes, so this takes many addresses. With `TRUST_PROXY=true` and no reverse proxy in front, a client can choose the address the limit sees.
- The traffic generator's `server-full` scenario.

**How to check.**

- **Global Overview**: _Rooms_ against the `maxRooms` value in the `listening` log line; _Rooms and players_ over time.
- **Security & Abuse**: _Room creations refused_, _Room creation by outcome_, _Rate-limit hits by scope_ (scope `room_create`).
- **Game Analytics**: _Players per room_. Many rooms with hardly any players point at rooms nobody uses.
- Log lines of the refusals:

  ```
  {service_name="kritzel"} |= "room refused"
  ```

**What to do.**

- Real demand: raise `MAX_ROOMS`. That needs a restart, which ends running games. Check memory first on **Runtime & Infrastructure**; the measured capacity in the [hosting guide](HOSTING.md) covers 30 rooms of 8 players, not 500 full rooms.
- Unused rooms: they expire on their own five minutes after the last participant left. Block the source at the reverse proxy if it continues, and check `TRUST_PROXY`.
- Synthetic traffic: stop it.

**Owner:** the operator of the instance.

## KritzelEventLoopLag

**Severity:** warning. **Rule:** `max(nodejs_eventloop_delay_p99_seconds) > 0.1` for 5 minutes.

**What it means.** The 99th percentile of the event-loop delay has been above 100 ms for five minutes. The server is one single-threaded process and every room shares its event loop, so drawing feels laggy for everyone. The sampling interval is already subtracted, so an idle process reports about zero.

**Likely causes.**

- More rooms and drawing traffic than one CPU core handles.
- The container is CPU-limited or shares a busy host.
- Long garbage-collection pauses, usually together with a nearly full heap.
- A flood of messages from broken or hostile clients.

**How to check.**

- **Runtime & Infrastructure**: _Event-loop delay_, _Event-loop utilisation_, _CPU by mode_ (sustained values near 1 core mean saturation), _Garbage collection pause, 95th percentile_, _Memory_.
- **WebSocket & Reliability**: _Messages by type_, _Processing duration by type, 95th percentile_, _Drawing relay duration_.
- **Global Overview**: _Rooms_ and _Players_. Does the delay follow the load?
- Is `KritzelWsLatencyBurn`, `KritzelHeapNearLimit` or `KritzelRateLimitSurge` firing as well?

**What to do.**

- Load: give the container more CPU, or lower `MAX_ROOMS` so the instance stops growing (restart needed). A second instance does not help.
- Garbage collection: see [KritzelHeapNearLimit](#kritzelheapnearlimit).
- A flood: see [KritzelRateLimitSurge](#kritzelratelimitsurge) and [KritzelRejectedMessageSurge](#kritzelrejectedmessagesurge).
- A restart clears the state and ends every game. Use it only when the process does not recover.

**Owner:** the operator of the instance.

## KritzelHeapNearLimit

**Severity:** warning. **Rule:** `max(v8js_memory_heap_used_bytes / v8js_memory_heap_limit_bytes) > 0.85` for 10 minutes.

**What it means.** The used V8 heap has been above 85 % of the heap limit for ten minutes. If the heap reaches the limit, Node.js stops with an out-of-memory error and every running game ends.

**Likely causes.**

- More rooms than the memory allows. Rooms and their drawings are kept in memory.
- A heap limit that is small for the workload.
- A memory leak: the heap grows while the number of rooms stays flat.

**How to check.**

- **Runtime & Infrastructure**: _Heap used_, _Memory_ (resident memory, heap used, heap limit), _Garbage collection time_.
- **Global Overview**: _Rooms and players_. Compare its shape with the heap.
- In Explore:

  ```
  max(v8js_memory_heap_used_bytes) / max(v8js_memory_heap_limit_bytes)
  ```

**What to do.**

- The heap follows the rooms: lower `MAX_ROOMS` or give the process more memory. Memory is freed when rooms are disposed. The heap limit is Node.js's own setting (`--max-old-space-size`); this repository does not set it.
- The heap grows without more rooms: plan a restart at a quiet moment, and report the graphs to the project.
- Both changes need a restart, which ends running games. A planned restart is better than an out-of-memory crash at the busiest moment.

**Owner:** the operator of the instance.

## KritzelRejectedMessageSurge

**Severity:** info. **Rule:** more than 20 % of inbound WebSocket messages rejected and more than one message per second in total, over 5 minutes, for 10 minutes.

**What it means.** An unusual share of messages is answered with an error. Some rejections are ordinary; one in five on a busy instance is not.

**Likely causes.**

- Browsers still running an old version of the web app after an update (`version_mismatch`, `bad_request`).
- A defect in the web app that sends messages the server refuses.
- A broken or hostile client: frames that are no known message (type `invalid`), `bad_request`, `invalid_op`.
- A client that sends faster than a rate limit (`rate_limited`).

**How to check.**

- **Security & Abuse**: _Rejected share_, _Rejected messages by reason_, _Rejected messages by type_.
- In Explore:

  ```
  sum by (kritzel_reason, kritzel_ws_message_type) (rate(kritzel_messages_rejected_total[5m]))
  ```

- The log line `message rejected` exists at `LOG_LEVEL=debug` only.

**What to do.**

- Usually nothing. The server protects itself: a connection that has 50 messages dropped within ten seconds (malformed, sent before joining, or over a rate limit) is closed.
- It started with an update: players need to reload the page. If reloading does not help, roll back to the previous image.
- A hostile source: block it at the reverse proxy.

**Owner:** the operator of the instance.

## KritzelRateLimitSurge

**Severity:** info. **Rule:** more than 2 rate-limit refusals per second for one scope, for 10 minutes. The alert's label `kritzel_scope` names the scope.

**What it means.** One rate limit is hit continuously. The limits are doing their job; the question is who keeps running into them.

| Scope         | Limit                                                              |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `draw`        | Drawing messages per connection: 90 per second                     |
| `chat`        | Chat messages per connection: a burst of five, then one per second |
| `room_create` | Ten room creations per minute per address                          |
| `telemetry`   | 30 browser measurement reports per minute per address              |

**Likely causes.**

- `draw` or `chat`: a script, or a defective client that sends too fast.
- `room_create` or `telemetry`: a script; or many players behind one address. Behind a reverse proxy without `TRUST_PROXY=true`, every player shares the proxy's address and its limit.

**How to check.**

- **Security & Abuse**: _Rate-limit hits_, _Rate-limit hits by scope_, _Room creation by outcome_, _Browser measurement reports_.
- The `listening` log line shows `trustProxy`.

**What to do.**

- Behind a proxy, set `TRUST_PROXY=true` (restart needed).
- A hostile source: block it at the reverse proxy.
- Scope `telemetry`: only measurements are lost. No action is needed for the game.

**Owner:** the operator of the instance.

## KritzelTelemetryDropped

**Severity:** info. **Rule:** `sum(rate({__name__=~"otel_sdk_processor_(span|log)_processed_total", error_type="queue_full"}[5m])) > 0` for 10 minutes.

**What it means.** One of the game's export queues (2048 spans, 2048 log records) is full, and new items are dropped. Gameplay is unaffected. Traces or exported logs are incomplete, and the span metrics and their exemplars undercount. It can fire only while metrics still arrive; when nothing arrives, `KritzelTelemetryMissing` fires instead.

**Likely causes.**

- The collector is slow, or its memory limiter refuses data.
- Tempo is down or slow, and the collector's queue towards it is full.
- The game produces more spans than it can export, under very high load.

**How to check.**

- **Runtime & Infrastructure**: _Spans dropped by the game_, _Export queues of the game_, _Collector: refused or failed_, _Collector: export queues_, _Reachable_, _Memory by component_.
- The game's stdout for `telemetry export problem`.

**What to do.**

- Repair or relieve the collector or the backend. The game needs no restart.
- If the volume is the cause, sample traces: `OTEL_TRACES_SAMPLER=parentbased_traceidratio` with `OTEL_TRACES_SAMPLER_ARG` (restart needed).
- Nothing here is urgent for players.

**Owner:** the operator of the instance.

## KritzelJoinSuccessBurn

**Severity:** critical (page) or warning (ticket). **Objective:** `join-success`, 99.9 % of valid joins succeed.

**What it means.** Joins that were not the player's or the host's mistake are being refused faster than the error budget allows. Players cannot get into rooms.

**Likely causes.**

- A defect in the server or the web app, typically right after an update.
- A join that the server refused without a specific error code (recorded as `bad_request`).
- On a quiet instance: one or two failures among few joins.

**How to check.**

- **Global Overview**: _Valid joins succeeding_, _Joins by outcome_.
- **Security & Abuse**: _Refused joins by reason_. This panel includes the excluded reasons; the query below does not.
- In Explore, the failures by reason and their absolute number:

  ```
  sum by (kritzel_reason, kritzel_role) (increase(kritzel_join_attempts_total{kritzel_validity="valid", kritzel_outcome="rejected"}[1h]))
  ```

- **WebSocket & Reliability**: _Recent join traces_. A refused valid join has a `room.join` span with error status; in Explore with Tempo:

  ```
  { name = "room.join" && status = error }
  ```

**What to do.**

- A handful of failures on a quiet instance: note the reason; no action.
- It started with an update: roll back to the previous image. The restart ends running games, but new players cannot join anyway.
- Otherwise: keep the reason and a trace, and report it to the project.

**Owner:** the operator of the instance.

## KritzelWsLatencyBurn

**Severity:** critical (page) or warning (ticket). **Objective:** `ws-processing-latency`, 99 % of inbound WebSocket messages are processed within 100 ms.

**What it means.** Too many messages take longer than 100 ms on the server, from reading the frame to queuing the answers. Players notice a delay between drawing and seeing. The objective says nothing about the network.

**Likely causes.**

- The causes of [KritzelEventLoopLag](#kritzeleventlooplag): CPU saturation, CPU limits, garbage collection.
- One slow message type.

**How to check.**

- **WebSocket & Reliability**: _Processed within 100 ms_, _Processing duration_, _Processing duration by type, 95th percentile_, _Message spans, 95th percentile_ (click a dot to open the trace of one slow message), _Drawing relay duration_.
- **Runtime & Infrastructure**: _Event-loop delay_, _CPU by mode_, _Garbage collection pause, 95th percentile_.

**What to do.**

- All types are slow: treat it as [KritzelEventLoopLag](#kritzeleventlooplag).
- One type is slow: keep a trace of it and report it to the project.

**Owner:** the operator of the instance.

## KritzelReconnectLatencyBurn

**Severity:** warning (ticket). There is no page variant. **Objective:** `reconnect-latency`, 99 % of reconnecting seats are back within 5 s.

**What it means.** Too many seats that resumed had been without a connection for more than five seconds. This is absence as the server saw it. A player who closes the laptop and comes back within the seat's grace period counts as well, so this alert can reflect behaviour and not a fault.

**Likely causes.**

- An unstable network between the players and the server.
- A reverse proxy that closes WebSocket connections, for example through a short read timeout or a reload.
- Players leaving and returning on their own.
- The web app's reconnect attempts back off (0.5, 1, 2, 4, then 5 s); a seat whose first attempts fail is away for longer than five seconds.

**How to check.**

- **WebSocket & Reliability**: _Reconnected within 5 s_, _Time away before reconnecting_, _Departures by cause_. Many `connection_lost` at the same moment across the instance point at the proxy or the network; a steady trickle points at players.
- The same dashboard, _Join and reconnect as measured in browsers_, if browser measurements are offered. It covers only players who switched them on.
- Your reverse proxy's timeouts and logs. The [hosting guide](HOSTING.md) shows a read timeout of one hour for nginx.

**What to do.**

- Losses in bursts: fix the proxy or the network path. The game needs no restart.
- Ordinary behaviour of your players: the target may be too strict for your instance. Change it in `deploy/helm/kritzel-observability/files/slo/kritzel.slo.yaml` and run `pnpm slo:generate`.

**Owner:** the operator of the instance.

## KritzelApiAvailabilityBurn

**Severity:** critical (page) or warning (ticket). **Objective:** `api-availability`, 99.9 % of requests to `/api/*` are answered without a server error.

**What it means.** The HTTP API answers with `5xx` faster than the error budget allows. The API creates rooms, looks rooms up, tells the web app its configuration and accepts browser measurement reports. Running games use the WebSocket and are not affected directly.

**Likely causes.**

- The server is full: `POST /api/rooms` answers `503`. `KritzelServerFull` fires as well.
- An unexpected error in a request handler (`500`).
- The traffic generator's `server-full` scenario.
- On a quiet instance: few requests, so a few errors are a large share.

**How to check.**

- **Global Overview**: _API availability_, _HTTP server errors by route_.
- **Security & Abuse**: _HTTP client and server errors_, _Warnings and errors_.
- In Explore, the errors by route and status:

  ```
  sum by (http_route, http_response_status_code) (rate(http_server_request_duration_seconds_count{http_route=~"/api/.*", http_response_status_code=~"5.."}[5m]))
  ```

- Traces of failed requests, in Explore with Tempo. A request answered with `5xx` has a span with error status:

  ```
  { resource.service.name = "kritzel" && status = error }
  ```

**What to do.**

- `503` on `/api/rooms`: follow [KritzelServerFull](#kritzelserverfull).
- `500`: if it started with an update, roll back to the previous image. Otherwise keep the route, the trace and its `error.type`, and report it to the project.

**Owner:** the operator of the instance.
